# Policies and support

## Teams and captains

Teams for the final assignment are five, self-formed, and choose their own
captain; where the cohort is not a multiple of ten the tutors fill the empty
seats so every team fields five and every team has an opponent.

## The Hall of Fame carries no marks

The most valuable player from each winning side is listed on the
[Hall of Fame](/hall-of-fame/) page, which is a wall of names and nothing
else — it carries no marks, no bonus and no weight in any assignment.

## The patch is pinned to 7.41f

Every figure on this site comes from patch 7.41f, and a patch released
mid-semester does not change any marking criterion; where the live game and
this site disagree, the assignment you are being marked against is the one
described here.

## If you cannot make match day

The same mechanism used to fill an uneven cohort covers this too: **a
missing player is not a forfeit and not a rescheduled series** — a tutor
takes the empty seat and the match is played.

What you lose is the participation portion of Assignment 4, the smallest of
its three parts. The team review and your individual reflection are still
yours to write, and still marked. Tell your tutor **before the week**, not
after — a seat has to be arranged, and that cannot happen once match day has
already started.

## If a game turns abusive

Nothing this course asks of you happens in a public game. Every Lobby Lab is
a bot match, a practice lobby, a replay, or a custom game against your own
classmates; Assignment 1 is practice mode, Assignment 2 is a replay,
Assignment 3 is written, and Assignment 4 is a custom lobby against another
team from this course. The course never puts you in a queue with strangers.

If you play publicly on your own time, mute, report through the game's own
tools, and leave if you want to — no page on this site asks you to finish a
game that has turned on you. If something happens in a course lobby, or
something outside the course is affecting your work in it, tell your tutor.
Not because there is a procedure to invoke, but because they can change what
the week asks of you.

## On speed, and on hands

Of the four assignments, exactly one asks for a mechanical result, and it is
worth the least of the four. The other three ask what you decided, what you
noticed in a replay, and whether you can explain a hero to somebody who has
never played it. The course is built that way because what it teaches is
decisions, and a decision does not become less correct for being executed
slowly.

Assignment 1 is marked on **improvement against the baseline you recorded in
Lobby Lab 2**, not an absolute count — your starting number was never a mark,
only a place to measure from. That also means the course does not need a
separate accommodation for hands that do not move quickly: the assignment
was never measuring speed, it was measuring the distance you travelled.

If something about how your hands work makes a particular drill unworkable
anyway, say so to your tutor and the drill gets changed. The mechanic being
taught nearly always survives being practised differently.

## Extensions

Email [Wen Adeyemi](/people/wen-adeyemi/), the convenor, before the deadline.
Short extensions are routine and you do not need to be in a crisis to ask for
one — you need to ask before the thing is due rather than after.
