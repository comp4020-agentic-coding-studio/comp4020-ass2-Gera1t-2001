## This week's decision

The lane stops being the only place worth being. Pulling, stacking and the
camps in the space between lanes are all ways of spending time somewhere other
than where the experience is, and each one is a small bet that what comes back
is worth what you gave up.

The mistake this week fixes is leaving the lane "to have a look". Beginners do
not fail by being afraid to leave — they fail by leaving without a reason. A
rotation with no target trades certain experience for nothing at all.

## Mechanics

**Camps spawn on a rhythm, and only when empty.** So *when there is something
in the jungle* is predictable rather than lucky, and predictable things can be
planned around.

**Pulling.** Drawing neutrals into the creep wave so they clear it for you, or
so that you take creeps and neutrals at the same time.

**Stacking.** Pulling a camp out before it checks to respawn, so that a second
group spawns alongside the first. This is a pure trade of attention for gold —
no risk in it at all, only focus.

**Blocking.** Placing a ward so that a camp cannot spawn. Week 9 returns for
the other half of what wards do.

**The courier.** It decouples "I need this item" from "I must go home", which
is what makes staying in lane a decision rather than an endurance test.

## The resource lens

Time traded for experience. Leaving the lane means giving up a certain share
of experience for a possibly larger return — jungle gold, a rotation, a camp
you stacked two minutes ago. The trade is only good when you can name what you
went to get.

## This week's hero

**Lion — Earth Spike.** The most common payoff for leaving your lane is
catching somebody standing in the wrong place, and Earth Spike is the plainest
tool for it. It also makes a point this course will keep making: a support
leaving lane is not giving up farm. They are going to earn it somewhere else.
