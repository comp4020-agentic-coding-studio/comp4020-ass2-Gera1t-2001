## This week's decision

Last week you learned to take gold off a creep. This week is about the fact
that the place where you do it will not hold still. A wave has a balance
point, that point drifts, and both sides can move it deliberately — so the
ground you are standing on was chosen, either by you or for you.

The mistake this week fixes is standing at the front of the creep wave. It
looks like participating. It is taking creep aggro and enemy spells at the
same time, and pushing the lane while you do it.

## Mechanics

**Where the lane sits is decided by how fast each side's creeps die.** Every
extra attack you land moves it toward the enemy. Nothing about this is
mysterious once you know to look for it.

**Holding near your own tower is the beginner-friendly state.** You are close
to your tower, far from their support, and your escape is short.

**When pushing is right.** You are going home, you are going to the jungle, or
you want the enemy taking their last hits under their own tower. Pushing is
not the error; unconscious pushing is.

**Positioning.** Where you stand decides whether the enemy can hit you without
taking tower damage, and whether creep aggro works for you or against you.

**Where the waves meet is not the middle of the lane.** In mid it is: the two
sides meet at the centre of the map. In a safe lane the meeting point sits
*closer to the safe-lane team's own tier-1 tower* — which means the off-laner
across from them meets the wave far from their own tower and close to the
enemy's.

That asymmetry is the structural reason the off lane is the hard lane. It is
not a matter of the off-laner being outnumbered or the hero being weaker; the
neutral resting place of the wave is simply somewhere worse for them, before
anybody has done anything.

## The resource lens

Space traded for safety. Holding the lane deliberately gives up map space in
exchange for safety and clean last hits. You will spend the rest of the game
making versions of this trade; this is the first time you make it on purpose.

## This week's hero

**Wraith King — Vampiric Spirit.** It is the ability-shaped answer to "how do
I stay in this lane longer": lifesteal lets you trade health for last hits,
and the skeletons give you some say over where the wave sits. Set it beside
week 2's Dragon Blood and the comparison is the point — two different
solutions to the same problem, and which one you prefer says something about
how you want to play.

## This week's item

**Magic Wand.** The first item that reads the enemy. Its charges come from the
enemy spending spells, so it turns "what is the enemy doing" into something
you can bank and spend later. Week 9's information lens shows its face here
for the first time.

## After this week you can…

- say where the wave will settle on a given lane before it gets there, and why
  the two sides differ
- give a reason to push that is not "I want the tower"
- explain why the off lane is the hard lane in terms of distance, not skill
