## This week's decision

Gold is arriving and you have some sense of what your hero wants to become.
The shop is where those two meet, and it is the first place where a decision
you make in a menu changes what you are able to do on the map.

The mistake this week fixes runs in both directions: saving for one big item
while owning nothing in the meantime, or the reverse — spending everything on
components and never completing anything at all.

## Mechanics

**How the shop is structured.** Components, recipes, upgrades. Dota items are
assembled rather than selected, which is why an item you are halfway through
is already doing something for you.

**Consumables and the teleport scroll are not spending; they are
infrastructure.** They buy time on the map and the ability to be somewhere
else, which is a different kind of purchase from a damage item.

**The logic of a build.** Solve staying alive, then solve damage, then solve
whatever the enemy has aimed specifically at you.

**Follow the recommended build first, and change it once you understand why.**
That is this course's position and we will not apologise for it. An item you
cannot justify is worse than one somebody else chose for you.

## The resource lens

Gold into stats. Week 2 said gold is time; week 5 said attributes are the
exchange rate. This week joins them up: your gold passes through that rate and
comes out as specific numbers, and numbers only matter inside particular
windows of a game.

## Before the lecture

Valve's in-game tutorial, section 1 — regeneration and teleporting; and
section 4 — build guides and the item panel.

## This week's hero

**Lich — Frost Shield.** Lich's mana pressure is severe enough that a student
feels, for the first time, that what they bought directly decides how many
spells they get to cast. Items and abilities stop being two separate subjects
here and start being one.

## This week's item

**Power Treads.** The first item that asks you to *operate* it during the
game, by switching which attribute it is giving you. It turns buying from a
one-off decision into a running one, which is exactly the step this week wants
you to take.

A note on in-game guides: open one from your first game. TorteDeLini's build
guides answer "what do I buy and when" for you, which frees your attention for
everything else on this page. Some time after week eleven you will start
disagreeing with the guide. That is the point at which you have graduated.

## After this week you can…

- play a whole game following a guide, and say where you wanted to deviate
- explain what each item in your starting kit is for
- say what to buy when the enemy has aimed something at you
