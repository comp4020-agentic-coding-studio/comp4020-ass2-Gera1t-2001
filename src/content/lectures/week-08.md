## This week's decision

Every week so far has been about acquiring something. This one is about the
fact that a hero who cannot act is worth nothing regardless of what they
acquired. Control is what turns a strong position into a lost fight, and like
everything else in this game it is bought and sold.

The question is phrased negatively on purpose. Beginners lose most of their
games to fights that were already decided before they started, and learning to
recognise that shape is worth more right now than learning to win the fights
themselves.

The mistake this week fixes is spending a control spell the moment it comes
off cooldown — or buying a Black King Bar and walking into five people
believing you are invulnerable.

## Mechanics

**Kinds of control** — stuns, roots, silences, hexes — and **what each one
forbids**, which matters far more than how long it lasts.

**Status effects and dispels.** Some control can be removed and some cannot,
and that difference decides whether you owe an item slot to a dispel.

**Spell immunity is not invulnerability.** It means: for this window, part of
the enemy's toolkit does not apply to me. Knowing which part is the whole
skill.

**Evasion and armour.** Physical and magical damage need different answers,
and buying the wrong answer feels like buying nothing at all.

**The real subject is when not to open.** A beginner's first instinct on
acquiring a control spell is to spend it immediately. A control spell's value
is largely in the enemy having to account for it while you are still holding
it.

## The resource lens

A Black King Bar buys seconds of certainty. In a game where everyone is
gambling, that is the purest thing money can buy here, and it is the course's
most naked trade of gold for a resource that is not gold.

## This week's hero

**Sand King — Burrowstrike.** It is an initiation tool, and this week's
subject is the opposite of initiating. Teaching "when not to open" with an
opening tool in your hand is the week's teaching structure rather than an
accident of hero choice.

## This week's item

**Black King Bar** buys your own certainty, as above.

**Glimmer Cape** buys somebody else's: you spend your gold so that a teammate
survives. It belongs on the same line as week 6's Arcane Boots — who does this
item's benefit belong to?

## After this week you can…

- describe one time you meant to open and did not, and why
- look at the enemy lineup and say whether you need a Black King Bar
- tell apart a fight lost to being controlled and one lost to standing in the
  wrong place
