## This week's decision

Attributes tell you what a hero converts gold into. Roles tell you how much
gold a hero is expected to get, and between them they are most of what a draft
is arguing about.

The mistake this week fixes is five players who all want position one — or,
more quietly, picking a support and then playing it like a core, competing
with your own team for the same gold.

We think you should start on position five. Not because it is easier, because
it is not. A support's decisions surface soonest and cost the team least while
you are still learning to make them: where to ward, when to rotate, whose life
to spend yours on. Every one of those is a decision you can name afterwards,
and naming decisions afterwards is the only skill this course is actually
teaching. You still learn to last-hit in week 2, because every position needs
it.

The disagreement here is real and worth knowing about. Most beginner guides
say support, for roughly our reasons. A serious minority say mid — a
self-contained game, no dependence on four other people, and the most direct
feedback available. Both cases hold up. Ours is the one this course is
arranged around, and week 6's Lobby Lab is where you get to argue with it.

## Mechanics

**Positions one to five are an order of resource priority**, not a job
description. Position one takes the most gold, position five the least, and
everything else follows from that.

**Core against support.** A core converts the team's resources into late-game
power. A support spends their own time protecting that conversion. Neither is
the senior partner.

**Power spikes.** Every hero has a window where they are at their strongest,
and a team's plan is built around whose window is open right now.

**Why positions are agreed before picking**, rather than sorted out once the
game has started.

## The resource lens

How experience is distributed. Unlike gold, experience is shared inside a
radius — so who stands in the wave, which looks like an incidental detail, is
really the team allocating a resource. Week 4's "leaving the lane" now has a
team-level explanation rather than only a personal one.

## This week's hero

**Crystal Maiden — Brilliance Aura**, because it is the cleanest example of a
support whose value does not sit on their own hero. The beneficiaries are
other people, and the aura does its work whether or not Crystal Maiden is
doing anything interesting.

**Juggernaut — Blade Fury**, because it lets a position one farm safely very
early: a core spending resources on its own safety, made concrete.

## This week's item

**Arcane Boots.** The first item bought for the team rather than for yourself.
It forces a question the rest of the course keeps returning to — who does this
item's benefit belong to?
