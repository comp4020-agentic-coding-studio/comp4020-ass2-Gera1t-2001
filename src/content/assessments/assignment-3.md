## The brief

> Pick a hero. Write the guide you wish you had found in week 1.

Every weekly page in this course teaches at most two abilities, and says why
those two and not the rest. That restraint is a teaching decision — a beginner
handed a full kit reads it as a list and remembers none of it — but it leaves
something owed, and this is where the course pays it back. Here you do the
whole hero, and you do it yourself.

The guide has to argue. Dota already has a great many hero guides that are
build orders with the reasoning removed, and reproducing one is not this
assignment: anyone can tell you what to buy, and the in-game guides will do it
while you play. What you are being marked on is the *because*. Why this item
before that one, given what the hero cannot do until it arrives. Why this
ability first, given what the lane looks like in the first few minutes. Why a
different opening makes sense against a particular kind of opponent, and what
you give up by choosing it.

Choose a hero you have actually played. A guide written from the outside is
recognisable within a paragraph — it is fluent about the kit and silent about
what the hero feels like to pilot when it is not working, and the second thing
is what a reader in week 1 needs.

Week 7's stance applies here too, turned around. There we said: follow the
recommended build first, then understand why. This assignment is the *then*.
You are allowed to arrive at the same build the guide recommends. You are not
allowed to arrive at it without being able to say what it is for.

## What you submit

- A written guide covering: the hero's full kit, a skill order, an item build,
  and a laning plan.
- Sources for every figure you quote. The course pins its numbers to one
  patch; yours should be pinned to something too, and the guide should say
  what.

Format is yours. A guide is a document with a reader, so structure it for
someone reading it before their first game on the hero, not for a marker.

## How it is marked

The full kit is the floor, not the mark — describing all the abilities
correctly is what makes the guide complete, not what makes it good. The mark
is in the justifications: whether each build and skill-order choice is tied to
something the hero can or cannot do, whether the laning plan says what beats
it as well as what it wants, and whether the guide would survive contact with
a reader who then went and played the hero.
