# Who goes first?

Week 11

---

## Teamfights, last

This is where the course disagrees most with how Dota is usually taught.

A fight is the most legible thing in a match — and the worst place to begin.

Nearly everything that decides one was settled earlier.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> By the draft. By who had vision.
> By who could not be stopped from acting.

---

## The habit this week breaks

Going in first because you have the most health.

Initiation is decided by which abilities **combine** — not by who is hardest
to kill.

# Who opens?

---

## Initiation and counter-initiation

"Who opens" really means:

**whose combination of abilities pays most while the enemy has not reacted
yet?**

---

## The seconds before

Where everyone stands before a fight starts decides most of its outcome.

It is the part nobody is watching.

---

## Buyback

A mistake you can purchase back.

Once. Expensively.

---

## High ground

Attacking uphill is a structural disadvantage.

Vision and terrain — both against you.

# The draft

---

## Captains Mode, in concept

A **ban** handles what you cannot beat.

**Pick order** decides who is answering whom.

A **flexible hero** is a resource: it delays the moment you reveal your plan.

The Lobby Lab walks you through a real one.

---

## Whose game is this?

Week 6 put power spikes on one hero.

This week puts them between two teams.

When to fight and how to draft turn out to be the same question, asked twice.

---

## This week's hero

**Tidehunter** — *Ravage*

The textbook shape of initiation: wide, forgiving, and its consequences are
visible at once.

You can learn the judgement without the execution getting in the way.

---

## This week's item

**Refresher Orb.** A second attempt.

In 7.41f it refreshes abilities only — not items.

That makes what it buys unambiguous.
