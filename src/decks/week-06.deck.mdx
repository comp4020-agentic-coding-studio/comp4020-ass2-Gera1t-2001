# What am I for in this team?

Week 6

---

## Two questions, one draft

Attributes told you what a hero turns gold **into**.

Roles tell you how much gold a hero is **expected to get**.

Between them, that is most of what a draft is arguing about.

---

## The habit this week breaks

Five players who all want position one.

Or, more quietly: picking a support and playing it like a core —
competing with your own team for the same gold.

# Positions are a priority order

---

## One to five is not a job description

Position one takes the most gold. Position five takes the least.

Everything else follows from that order.

---

## Core and support

A **core** turns the team's resources into late-game power.

A **support** spends their own time protecting that conversion.

Neither is the senior partner.

---

## Power spikes

Every hero has a window where they are at their strongest.

A team's plan is built around **whose window is open right now**.

That is why positions are agreed before picking — not sorted out once the
game has started.

# Where we stand

---

## Start on position five

Not because it is easier. It is not.

A support's decisions surface soonest and cost the team least while you are
still learning to make them:

where to ward, when to rotate, whose life to spend yours on.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Naming your decisions afterwards is the only skill this course is actually
> teaching.

A support's decisions are the easiest to name.

---

## The other side of the argument

Most beginner guides say support, for roughly our reasons.

A serious minority say **mid**: a self-contained game, no dependence on four
other people, the most direct feedback there is.

Both cases hold up. Ours is the one this course is built around —
and the Lobby Lab is where you argue with it.

---

## Experience is shared

Gold goes to whoever takes the last hit.

Experience is split between everyone standing near the wave.

So *who stands in the wave* is not a detail. It is the team allocating a
resource.
