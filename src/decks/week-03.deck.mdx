# The wave has a balance point

---

## Who moves it

Where the lane sits is decided by how fast each side's creeps die.

Every extra attack you land moves it toward **their** tower.

Every deny, every attack you hold back, pulls it toward **yours**.

Nothing mysterious — once you know to look.

---

## Hold it near your tower

The beginner-friendly state.

Close to your tower. Far from their support. A short walk home.

You give up map. You get clean last hits and a way out.

---

## Pushing is not the error

Push when you are going home.
Push when you are going to the jungle.
Push when you want them taking last hits under their own tower.

**Unconscious** pushing is the error.

# The middle is not the middle

---

## Why the off lane is the hard lane

In mid, the waves meet at the centre of the map.

On a safe lane they meet **near the safe-laner's own tower** —

so the off-laner meets the wave far from home and close to the enemy.

Nobody outplayed them. The wave simply rests somewhere worse, before anyone
has done anything.

---

## Space traded for safety

Holding the lane gives up map space.

In exchange: safety, and clean last hits.

You will make versions of this trade for the rest of the game.
This is the first time you make it on purpose.

---

## This week's hero

**Wraith King** — *Vampiric Spirit*

Lifesteal lets you trade health for last hits.
The skeletons give you a say over where the wave sits.

Put it next to week 2's Dragon Blood:
two answers to "how do I stay longer". Which one you prefer says something
about how you want to play.

## This week's item

**Magic Wand.**

Its charges come from the enemy casting spells.

It turns *what are they doing* into something you can bank and spend later —
the first item that reads the enemy.
