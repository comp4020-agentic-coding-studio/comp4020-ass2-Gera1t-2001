# What do I buy?

Week 7

---

## Where gold meets a plan

Gold is arriving. You know roughly what your hero wants to become.

The shop is where those two meet —

the first place where a decision made in a menu changes what you can do on
the map.

---

## The habit this week breaks

It runs both ways:

saving for one big item while owning nothing in the meantime —

or spending everything on components and never finishing anything.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Items are assembled, not chosen

---

## Components, recipes, upgrades

Dota items are built from parts.

So an item you are halfway through is already doing something for you.

---

## Bought for what they let you keep doing

**Consumables** keep you in the lane you are already in.

**The teleport scroll** means a lane is never lost just because you are not
standing in it.

Neither adds anything. Both keep you in the game.

---

## The order of a build

1. Solve staying alive.
2. Then solve damage.
3. Then solve whatever the enemy has aimed at **you**.

# Follow the guide first

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> An item you cannot justify is worse than one somebody else chose for you.

This is the course's position, and we will not apologise for it.

---

## Open a guide from your first game

TorteDeLini's in-game build guides answer *what do I buy, and when*.

That frees your attention for everything else.

Some time after week eleven you will start disagreeing with the guide.
By then, you will have reasons.

---

## Gold into stats

Week 2: gold is time.
Week 5: attributes are the exchange rate.

This week joins them — your gold passes through that rate, and what comes out
only matters inside particular windows of a game.

---

## This week's hero

**Lich** — *Frost Shield*

Lich's mana pressure is severe enough that you feel it:

what you bought decides how many spells you get to cast.

Items and abilities stop being two subjects here.

---

## This week's item

**Power Treads.**

The first item you **operate** during the game, by switching which attribute
it gives you.

Buying stops being a one-off decision and becomes a running one.
