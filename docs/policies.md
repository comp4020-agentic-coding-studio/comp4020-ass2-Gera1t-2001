Slop University is fictional. There is no institutional extensions office, no
accommodations service and no student-conduct process behind this page — the
course is the institution, and the convenor is where the buck stops. So **do
not point at a process that does not exist**, and do not write a sentence
whose shape implies one ("in line with University policy", "through the
usual channels"). Every route on this page ends at a named person on

## 1. If you cannot make match day

The mechanism already exists and the page should simply use it: **the course
already fills empty seats with tutors** when the cohort is not a multiple of
ten. So a missing player is not a forfeit and not a rescheduled series — a
tutor takes the seat and the match is played.

What the student loses is the participation portion of Assignment 4 and
nothing else: the team review and the individual reflection are both still
theirs to write and to be marked on. Say that plainly, including that it is
the smallest of the three parts.

Tell your tutor **before the week**, not after. The point of the notice is
that a seat has to be arranged, and that cannot happen retroactively.

## 2. If a game turns abusive

Lead with the course's own design, because it is a stronger answer than any
advice: **nothing this course asks of you happens in a public game.** Every
Lobby Lab is a bot match, a practice lobby, a replay or a custom game against
your own classmates. Assignment 1 is practice mode, Assignment 2 is a replay,
Assignment 3 is written, and Assignment 4 is a custom lobby against another
team from this course. The course never puts you in a queue with strangers.

Then the rest, briefly: if you play publicly on your own time, mute and report
through the game's own tools and leave if you want to — no page on this site
asks you to finish a game that has turned on you. If something happens in a
course lobby, or something outside the course affects your work in it, tell
your tutor. Not because there is a procedure, but because they can change
what the week asks of you.

## 3. On speed, and on hands

This is the section that says what kind of course this is, so it gets written
carefully and without hedging.

The honest framing, which is **checkable against the assessment pages** and
not a comforting claim: of the four assignments, exactly one asks for a
mechanical result, and it is worth the least of the four. The other three ask
what you decided, what you noticed in a replay, and whether you can explain a
hero to somebody who has never played it. The course is arranged that way
because the thing it teaches is decisions, and a decision does not become
less correct for being executed slowly.

**Assignment 1 changes to match.** It marks **improvement against the baseline
you recorded in Lobby Lab 2**, not an absolute count. Lobby Lab 2's page
already tells students that the number they leave with is a starting point
and not a mark; this is that sentence carried through to the assignment. It
also means the course does not need a separate accommodation for hands that
do not move quickly — the assignment was never measuring speed, it was
measuring the distance you travelled.

If something about how your hands work makes a particular drill unworkable
anyway, say so to your tutor and the drill gets changed. The mechanic being
taught nearly always survives being practised differently.

**This supersedes the open question about A1's threshold.** There is no
absolute last-hit count to publish. Update the Assignment 1 page to match,
and say in your report that you did.

## 4. Extensions

Email **Wen Adeyemi**, the convenor, before the deadline. Short extensions are
routine and you do not need to be in a crisis to ask for one; you need to ask
before the thing is due rather than after.

Link her name to `/people/wen-adeyemi/`. Do not invent a form, a portal or a
committee.
